Subject: Project Vari - The Master Plan 🚀
Hey team,

Here is the high-level summary of what we are building for our Major Project, Vari, and where we are at right now.

🌍 The Problem
Right now, rural water testing and disease tracking are completely disconnected. The government tests water, and doctors treat patients, but nobody connects the dots. By the time they realize a bad water source is making people sick, it’s already an outbreak.

💡 Our Solution: Vari
Vari is not just a data-entry app; it is an Epidemiological Early-Warning System. We are building a platform that allows ASHA (health) workers to log water quality and patient sickness at the exact same time, linked to a specific GPS location.

📱 How It Works (The Flow)

The App: An ASHA worker opens the Vari app at a village water source.

Auto-GPS: The app automatically locks their exact GPS coordinates.

Water Data: They enter the water's pH, TDS, and Turbidity.

Patient Data: If people are sick, the worker adds "Victims" to that specific report (Name, Age, Disease like Diarrhea/Typhoid, Contact, and a Photo).

The Brain: The Spring Boot backend receives this, links the patients to the water source in a relational database, and instantly flags the area as a "Red Zone" if it's unsafe.

🛠️ Our Tech Stack

Frontend: Flutter (Runs on Android, iOS, and Web)

Backend: Java Spring Boot (REST API)

Database: PostgreSQL hosted on Supabase Cloud

🔥 What We Have Built So Far

The Flutter app is successfully talking to the Spring Boot backend.

We have a working form with real-time GPS tracking.

We have relational database insertion working (Water Reports + Multiple Patients saving together).

A "History" dashboard that color-codes Safe (Green) and Unsafe (Red) zones.

🚀 Next Steps (How we make this an "A+" Project)
To make this government/startup ready, our next targets are:

Security: Implement real Login/Authentication for the ASHA workers.

AI Integration (Gemini): Send the data to an AI so it can predict outbreaks (e.g., "Warning: High risk of Cholera based on acidity and current patient symptoms").

Dashboarding: Build a heatmap view for officials to see where diseases are spreading.
